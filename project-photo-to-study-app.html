Example project

From a photo of a page to a study app in minutes.

A student needed to learn vocabulary from a printed textbook, one chapter at a time. DataSail built a phone app where a photo of the page becomes a practice deck: AI reads the page, a person checks it, and the new set is on every phone right away.

Problem statement

Printed material is slow to turn into practice.

Typing a chapter of vocabulary into a flashcard tool takes longer than studying it, so it rarely happens. The material changed every week, and practice needed to work on a phone anywhere, including with no signal.

Solution summary

Photograph it, let AI read it, review it.

Photos are shrunk on the phone and sent to an AI vision model, which extracts each word and its translation and flags problems such as glare, a cut-off list or an unreadable word. Nothing is saved until a person has reviewed and corrected it.

Value realized

A new chapter is ready to study the same evening.

Adding a chapter takes a few photos and a quick review instead of an hour of typing. The app installs to the home screen, pronounces each word, tracks starred and known words, and works in airplane mode.

How it generalizes

The same pattern fits paper forms, catalogs and checklists.

Anywhere information starts on paper, the flow is the same: capture it with a phone, have AI structure it, keep a person in charge of what gets saved, and put the result in a simple app people actually use.

Accuracy Checked

AI drafts the data and reports what it was unsure about, and a person approves every set before it goes live.

Cost control Rate limits

AI calls are capped per user and per day, so a leaked link can never run up a surprise bill.

Reliability Offline

The whole library is cached on the device, and an unreachable server never shows stale or broken pages.
